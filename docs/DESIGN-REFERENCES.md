# Design references: what our shells must avoid

Kayboro shells may take *general* inspiration from real consoles, for example "a portrait pocket handheld" or "a widescreen grip handheld". They must never copy a real product's **overall look**: its silhouette and proportions, its control layout, its button shapes and symbols, or its signature colourway. This page lists what is protected, or recognisable enough to count as trade dress, for the products our handheld shells are most likely to echo. Check new shells against it before shipping.

This is a design checklist, not legal advice. Several of the older design patents have expired, but a product's look can still be protected as trade dress, unregistered or registered, and by trademark registrations of a front panel or symbols. So treat every item below as off limits.

## How the protection works (short)

- **US design patents** protect the ornamental look shown in the drawings: "The ornamental design for a ... as shown and described". The term is 14 years from grant, or 15 years for applications filed from 13 May 2015. Infringement is judged by whether an ordinary observer would find the overall look substantially the same, so changing small details does not help if the whole still reads as the product.
- **Trade dress / design marks** protect a recognisable, non-functional look (shape, layout, colours together) for as long as it is used. This is why the Game Boy front panel is still protected decades later.
- **Trademarks** cover names, logos and symbol sets such as button glyphs, wordmarks and the "Nintendo"/"GAME BOY"/"PlayStation"/"Steam" names.

## Product by product

### Nintendo Switch and Joy-Con (design patents in force)

Nintendo's patent marking page lists the Switch's US design patents, including D815,696, D819,642, D819,696, D826,850, D826,949, D863,448, D875,178, D875,837, D876,550, D881,993 and D882,693. Joy-Con alone: D819,642, D863,448, D875,178, D875,837, D876,550, D882,693 and D1,027,040. D819,642 ("Controller for computer", granted 2018-06-05) and D863,448 (granted 2019-10-15) run into the 2030s.

Distinctive elements to avoid:
- A flat tablet slotted **between two detachable side rails** of equal height, with rounded outer corners and a flat inner edge.
- **Minus (-) button top-left and plus (+) button top-right**, a **round home button** low on the right rail and a **square capture button** low on the left rail.
- **Offset sticks**: left stick above the four direction buttons, right stick below the four face buttons.
- Four separate round direction buttons in place of a cross pad (left rail).
- The **neon red / neon blue split** colour scheme. We found no registered colour mark, but together with the layout it signals the product.

Sources: [Nintendo virtual patent marking](https://www.nintendo.com/patents), [USD819642S1](https://patents.google.com/patent/USD819642S1/en), [USD815696S1](https://patents.google.com/patent/USD815696S1/en), [USD863448S1](https://patents.google.com/patent/USD863448S1/en).

### Game Boy (DMG-01), Game Boy Pocket / Color

- US trademark Reg. No. 5,604,523 (serial 87632363, filed 2017, registered 2018-11-13) is a **design mark of the Game Boy front**. It describes a light grey rectangle with **one rounded corner** and an inner screen panel. It also covers the power and speaker legends, SELECT and START and the A and B buttons, with black, grey, blue, red and yellow claimed as features. So the original's front layout is a live registered mark, not just nostalgia.
- Distinctive elements to avoid: a portrait brick with the screen plate in the top half; the **single large curved bottom-right corner**; **slanted speaker slots in that corner**; a cross pad left with **two round buttons on a diagonal** to the right; **two slanted pill buttons** centred below; a "power ON/OFF" legend along the top edge; the **grey body with purple/magenta buttons and a dark-grey screen plate**.

Sources: [Justia trademark record 87632363](https://trademark.justia.com/876/32/nintendo-game-boy-select-start-a-87632363.html), [Game Boy (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Game_Boy).

### Game Boy Advance and Game Boy Advance SP

- Landscape body with the **screen centred**, a cross pad left, **two round buttons on a diagonal** right, shoulder buttons on rounded "horns", and a speaker grille low on the right. The original was indigo/purple.
- SP: a square **clamshell** with the screen in the lid and the pad and buttons on the base.
- Their 2001-2003 design patents have expired, but the layout is iconic trade dress. Avoid the pad-left plus two-diagonal-buttons-right landscape arrangement.

Source: [Game Boy Advance SP (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Game_Boy_Advance_SP).

### Nintendo DS / 3DS family

- A clamshell with **two stacked screens** and a barrel hinge, a cross pad left and a **four-button diamond** right on the lower half, and start/select below them. The 3DS family has active US design patents (for example D666,679 for the 3DS, D697,139 and following for the 3DS XL, D745,606 for the New 3DS and D720,404-407 for the 2DS), all listed on Nintendo's marking page.
- Avoid: a top screen plus a second screen on the base with pad left and diamond right.

Source: [Nintendo virtual patent marking](https://www.nintendo.com/patents).

### Sony PSP / PS Vita

- PSP: a glossy **piano-black stadium/oval landscape body** with a chrome rim, the screen filling the middle, a cross pad left and a **four-button diamond** right, an analog nub below the pad, and a row of small buttons under the screen. Vita: the same layout with two sticks under the clusters.
- The **triangle / circle / cross / square** glyphs are the PlayStation brand's best-known symbols. Sony uses them as a trademark and has registered controller shapes as 3D marks in several countries. Never put those four symbols on buttons, in any colour.

Sources: [World Trademark Review on Sony 3D marks](https://www.worldtrademarkreview.com/article/three-dimensional-marks-remain-tough-proposition), [Teiyu Goto profile (SuperJump)](https://superjumpmagazine.com/teiyu-goto-the-unsung-genius-behind-the-playstation).

### Valve Steam Deck

- Valve holds US design patent **D1,036,548** ("Electronic device", filed 2021-05-05, granted 2024-07-23), naming Steam Deck hardware designers as inventors. Its related Japanese filings are titled "electronic game machine". Valve also has utility patents on the trackpads' pressure sensing.
- Distinctive elements to avoid: a wide body with flared grips; the **thumbsticks at the top of each grip**; **square trackpads directly under the sticks**; the cross pad lower-left and the four face buttons lower-right, beside the screen; view and menu buttons beside the screen corners; the dark-grey body.

Sources: [USD1036548S1](https://patents.google.com/patent/USD1036548S1/en), [Steam Deck (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steam_Deck).

### Also off limits

- **Four coloured buttons in a diamond.** Red/yellow/green/blue (SNES) or the Xbox colour-and-letter coding both read as a specific brand.
- **Game & Watch**: a landscape metal-faced unit or clamshell with a cross pad left and one action button right, plus the "GAME & WATCH" wording.
- Any real wordmark, logo or legend: GAME BOY, Nintendo, PlayStation, Steam, SELECT/START, A/B/X/Y letters arranged as on a real pad.

## What our shells do instead

Use original control vocabularies:
- a round dial pad with a dimple instead of a cross
- a crescent arc of two or three buttons
- vertical button towers
- scroll wheels and rotary knobs
- slider switches and toggle levers
- petal- or gem-shaped keys

Use original silhouettes:
- arched or tombstone tops
- chamfered octagons
- one-piece swept wing grips
- scalloped or cloud edges
- seashell or compact cases

Use original colour pairings that do not recall a product's launch colourway. Mixing *one* generic idea, such as "a cross pad exists" or "it's a clamshell", is fine. Reproducing a product's *combination* of silhouette, layout and colours is not.
